This repository's proof debt, in the four dispositions the trusted-base policy
allows. An escape hatch is either discharged, budgeted and tested, a necessary
axiom, or debt. There is no fifth category and no silent one.

The difference from most repositories' version of this file: everything below is
empty, and that emptiness is *enforced* rather than asserted. `scripts/check.sh`
refuses the build if any `.agda` file under `src/SecretTypes` or `tests`
contains `postulate`, and the same tree is compiled with `--safe`, which
independently refuses `postulate`, `{-# TERMINATING #-}`, `{-# NON_TERMINATING #-}`
and `--type-in-type`. The only postulates in the import cone are the four that
Agda itself supplies in `Agda.Primitive`, each bound by `BUILTIN`.
`scripts/check-trusted-base.sh` finds them in the cone and fails on any other.
`verification/receipts/agda_praxis.deed` lists them as its `(trusted-base ...)`
and records, beside each receipt, the axioms that the run reported.

== (a) Discharged in this repo

_None — and none ever needed._ No escape hatch was ever introduced here to be
later discharged: the development has been `--safe` since its first module, so
there is no historical debt that a reader would otherwise have to trust a
changelog about.

Evidence: `scripts/check.sh` step 0 (trusted base), `scripts/proof-flags.sh`
(the `--safe` flag and why), and the `[trust-base]` block of the receipt.

== (b) Budgeted — tested with refutation budget

_None._ No escape hatch is covered by tests with a refutation budget, because
there is no escape hatch.

Not to be confused with, and deliberately not filed here: the two *negative
controls* (`tests/reject/{ExplicitFlow,ImplicitFlow,ThirdVariable}`) and the
leak witnesses (`SecretTypes.Witness`). Those are checks of the typing rules,
not escapes from them — a fixture that type-checked would be a failure, and
`scripts/check.sh` fails if one is accepted, or if it fails for the wrong reason
(missing import, parse error, unsolved meta). Calling them "budgeted debt" would
misreport what they are.

== (c) Necessary axiom

_None._ No axiom is assumed, so nothing needs a justification, a citation or a
trust-boundary entry.

This is the load-bearing sentence for the repository's status claims: with no
postulates and no axioms in the trusted base, `PROOF-STATUS.adoc` may use the
ledger token `proven` rather than `proven-with-postulates`
(`standards/docs/README-EXPLAINME-STANDARD.adoc` §5, `PROOF-POLICY.adoc` §5). If
an axiom is ever needed, the procedure is fixed in advance: name it here with its
justification, list it in that claim's trust-boundary column in the
link:EXPLAINME.adoc#proof-ledger[Proof Ledger], and demote the token in the same
change. Each receipt records the axioms its run reported, and the gates refuse
any axiom, so the receipt cannot quietly keep saying none.

== (d) DEBT — actively to be closed

_None in the proof development._

Two items are open *obligations* rather than proof debt, and are listed here so
that they are not mistaken for either:

* *Recheck legs (`PWG-2ii`, met 2026-11-09).* `PROOF-POLICY.adoc` §2 asks for
  every Agda theory proof to be rechecked in Rocq and in Lean. Both legs now
  exist, re-proved by hand without a library or an axiom
  (link:../verification/legs/README.adoc[`verification/legs/README.adoc`]),
  and the receipt records them. The obligation that remains is review: the
  statement comparison was written by Claude Code and is provisional until the
  owner reviews it. The grade is what the pinned reader derives from the proof
  receipt (`PROOF-POLICY` §8.5.1); it needs the receipts of a CI run.
* *Termination-sensitive noninterference.*
  `Noninterference.OutcomeVisibility.termination-sensitive-fails` refutes TSNI
  for the current typing rules when finishing versus non-finishing is public.
  Designing and proving a stronger discipline, or specifying a different
  observer, remains an open research obligation rather than proof debt.

== Who checks this file

* `scripts/grade-receipt.sh --check` (CI: `.github/workflows/proofs.yml`, job
  `receipt`): the receipt must match the tree, the run ledger and the claim
  IDs in `PROOF-STATUS.adoc`, and the pinned reader must accept it.
* `scripts/check.sh`: `postulate` refusal, then the full gate.
* `scripts/check-trusted-base.sh`: the prover-supplied postulates of the
  import cone are exactly the receipt's `(trusted-base ...)`.

A future addition to this file that is not accompanied by a change in those three
places is a claim the gate does not support.

[[waivers]]
== Waivers — obligations this repository does not meet

None open. W-1 below is closed and kept for its evidence.

A waiver is not an escape hatch inside a proof and not proof debt. It is an
obligation the *policy* sets that this repository does not yet satisfy, recorded
in the place a reader will look. Each one names the obligation, the tool, and why
the mapped option was not used (`PROOF-POLICY.adoc` §4 asks for exactly that
shape when a mapped prover is not the right fit).

=== W-1 `--cubical-compatible` (PROOF-POLICY §2.1, dated 2026-11-01) — CLOSED 2026-11-09

*Closed.* The flag is in force in `scripts/proof-flags.sh`, and therefore in the
gate, the laws certificate and the receipt. The 13 remaining warnings below
(`just`-injectivity in `Noninterference.Generic`) were removed without arm B2: each
clause now reads the run's result through `Prelude.just-inj`, defined by `cong`
through the computing projection `from-just`, which needs no unification rule.
No statement, semantics or fixture changed. The flag is proved to be in force by
`tests/flag-control/IndexedMatch.agda`, a correct proof that only the flag
refuses; `scripts/check.sh` fails if it is ever accepted, and removing the flag
from `scripts/proof-flags.sh` was tested to fail the gate.

The premise of arm B2 below also turned out to be false. A relational semantics
does not by itself satisfy the flag: a probe proving determinism of
`c , s ⇓ t` raised 22 `UnsupportedIndexedMatch` warnings, because inverting two
derivations of one command needs injectivity of `Cmd`'s constructors. A big-step
relation is still planned, as a second semantics with an adequacy theorem
(`exec` finishes iff `⇓` holds), not as a replacement and not for this flag.

The record below is kept as it was written, for provenance.

*Obligation.* `agda --safe --without-K --cubical-compatible <module>` is the
canonical invocation for first-party Agda theory entrypoints.

*State.* `--safe` and `--without-K` are in the gate. `--cubical-compatible` is
**not**, and cannot be added without changing the development or weakening the
check.

*Evidence, from three CI runs on 2026-11-01 that adopted the flag and then had to
report why it would not stay.* The flag was added, the Agda pin was unchanged
(`2.6.4.3`, `agda-bin=2.6.4.3-1+b2`), and the gate went red three times:

. `Prelude.agda:43` `just-inj` — *"relies on injectivity of the data constructor
  `just`, which is not yet supported"*. **Fixed**: the lemma was dead code and
  was deleted.
. `Prelude.agda:91` `_≟_` — *"relies on injectivity of the data constructor
  `suc`"*. **Fixed**: `suc-dec` decides the predecessors and pulls the answer
  back along `pred`, which computes definitionally, so no injectivity rule is
  needed.
. `Lattice.agda:46` `⊑-trans` — the same warning class, this time because every
  clause that matches on the two-point order is an **indexed match** on `_⊑_`.
  **Not fixed.** This is not incidental: `_⊑_` is an indexed family with three
  constructors and no elimination principle other than matching, and `⊑-trans`,
  `⊔-upper₁`, `⊔-upper₂` and the `principal` observer all match on it. Cubical
  Agda does not support indexed matching; the warning says so and offers
  `-W[no]UnsupportedIndexedMatch`.

*Two arms, labelled — and one of them has since been taken part of the way.*

* *Arm A — keep the flag off and record this waiver.* The gate stays at
  `--safe --without-K --no-libraries --ignore-interfaces --double-check -W error`:
  four dimensions stricter than the canonical invocation, one flag absent. The
  absent flag is the one whose absence cannot hide an unsoundness here — it
  constrains compatibility with cubical transports, not the truth of the present
  theorems. The waiver stands, but it is **narrower than it was** (see below).
* *Arm B — re-represent so that nothing needs index unification.* Two parts:
  * *B1, done 2026-11-01.* `_⊑_` was an indexed family
    (`data _⊑_ : Label → Label → Set where L⊑L; L⊑H; H⊑H`), so matching any
    proof asked for injectivity of the index constructors `L`/`H`. It is now a
    **computed predicate** — `L ⊑ _ = ⊤`, `H ⊑ H = ⊤`, `H ⊑ L = ⊥` — with the
    laws proved by computation. The order still says exactly what it said, an
    impossible flow is still `⊥` (so the three expected-rejection fixtures still
    fail at their `⊑` obligation, checked), and the `H`-injectivity warnings are
    **gone**: 16 → 13. One cost, recorded at the refutation that needs it: a
    witness such as `L⊑H` no longer *pins* its target label by its type (since
    `L ⊑ y` is `⊤` for every `y`), so `Countermodels.NoDownClosure` names its
    indices explicitly (`h {x = L} {y = H}`).
  * *B2, not attempted.* The 13 remaining warnings are all
    `injectivity of the data constructor just`, in `tini`, `go`, `confine` and
    `confineW`. They come from matching an equation of the form
    `exec fuel c s ≡ just t`, where `exec` computes to `just v` and Agda must
    solve `t := v` — constructor injectivity, and the cubical-unsupported step.
    Two shortcuts were measured and do not work: a record-returning semantics
    warns on the *record* constructor instead (probe file, 2026-11-01), and
    threading the result as a variable fixes the equations it touches but not
    the ones inside the clauses. The technique that does work is to stop putting
    computed results in equality at all: restate the big-step semantics as a
    **relation** (`data c , s ⇓ t`) and match derivations, which unify a variable
    index against a constructor term instead of two constructor terms. That is a
    research change with a named consequence: **N-5** (termination-insensitivity
    is not vacuous) currently exhibits a program whose run returns `nothing`,
    a computation; under a relational semantics divergence is the *absence* of a
    derivation, so N-5 would need a new non-derivability argument. It also
    changes the shape of N-1/N-7. That is an owner decision about the statement
    of the theorem, not a refactor to slip into a flag change.
*What would make the waiver wrong.* If `-WUnsupportedIndexedMatch` were ever
suppressed in the gate, this waiver would become a lie — the flag would be
nominally on and the check off. The gate does not suppress it, and
`scripts/proof-flags.sh` is the single place the flags live, so that change
could not happen by accident.

*Consequence for the grade (as written on 2026-11-01, superseded).* It said the
grade was unaffected, under the brief's "Grade 3". `PROOF-POLICY` §8 (2026-11-04)
then required the §2.1 invocation for `PWG-3`, so while W-1 was open the theorems
were `ungraded`. Closing W-1 is what makes them `PWG-3`.
